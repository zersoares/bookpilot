Book Builder

From an idea to a finished book

Describe the book in a sentence. Approve the positioning, the structure and the voice. Write it chapter by chapter with a writer that has read your plan — and set it in a real interior design, with real pages.

  1. 1

    Idea

    One or two sentences. Ten titles, a promise, a reader and an angle come back. You choose.

  2. 2

    Blueprint

    Parts, chapters, word budgets that add up to the length you asked for, and where the exercises and figures belong. Drag to reorder.

  3. 3

    Manuscript

    Each chapter written against a Book Bible, so chapter nine still agrees with chapter two. Every draft you replace is kept.

  4. 4

    Design

    Eight interior designs, each a complete one: margins, running heads, chapter openers. The preview is the printed page, not a picture of it.

  5. 5

    Publish

    Print-ready PDF with a real gutter, EPUB for e-readers, Word for your editor, HTML for the web.

  6. 6

    Market

    A campaign written from the finished book — quoting the actual argument, naming the actual chapter. Then advertise it here too.

Eleven specialists, one book

Not one prompt doing everything. A Book Architect designs the structure, a Book Writer writes it, an Editorial Director marks it, a Visual Director chooses the figures, a Cover Designer works on the cover, a Quality Controller checks it before it goes out.

Every one of them reads the same Book Bible first — the voice, the vocabulary, the claims, what each chapter already established. That is what makes it a book rather than a collection of essays about the same subject.

What it will not do

  • Invent a citation, a statistic or a study. Where a claim needs a source, it says so.
  • Invent your credentials, your clients or your results. It leaves a marked placeholder.
  • Claim a book will sell, reach a list, or be accepted anywhere.
  • Show you artwork that does not exist. A figure with no image is left out, not faked.
  • Overwrite your draft. A revision is a proposal beside your text until you accept it.

The usual way

Writer → editor → designer → illustrator → marketer → publisher

Six handovers, six schedules, six invoices.

FAQ

Questions worth asking first

Does BookPilot spend my advertising budget?

No. You connect your own Meta ad account, and Meta bills you directly. BookPilot creates campaigns paused; nothing spends until you launch it, and the advisor never raises a budget on its own.

Can it track my Amazon sales?

Not directly — nobody can. Amazon does not expose per-ad sales data to third-party tools. What BookPilot supports is Amazon Attribution: if you have an eligible Amazon Attribution account, its clicks, detail-page views, add-to-carts and purchases can be imported and reported alongside your ad spend. Amazon-attributed sales are always labelled separately from sales tracked on your own website.

Will the AI predict which ad will sell?

No, and be careful with any tool that says it can. Every generated creative gets a predicted-quality score — a judgement about hook, clarity and audience fit before it runs. What actually sells is decided by campaign data, which is why the analytics and the advisor exist.

Will it invent reviews or claim my book is a bestseller?

No. Every prompt is bound by rules that forbid inventing awards, bestseller status, review quotes, sales figures, endorsements or guaranteed outcomes. If you supply real review text, it can use that. If you don't, it writes copy that works without it.

What happens to my manuscript?

Sample text you upload is used to analyse your book and generate marketing material, and is stored in your account. It is never used to train a model, never shared with other users, and you can export or permanently delete everything from Settings at any time.

Do I need to know how Meta Ads works?

No. The campaign builder asks about your book, your reader and your budget, and translates that into the campaign, ad set and ad structure Meta expects. You can still open Ads Manager and change anything — it's your account.

Can I try it before signing up?

Yes. The demo workspace runs the whole product on a sample book, with every screen marked as demo data. No account, no card, nothing to cancel.

Is my data handled under GDPR?

The product is built for European users: data export, account deletion, consent records and privacy settings are in the app itself. See the privacy policy for the detail. It isn't legal advice, and it doesn't replace your own obligations as a controller of your readers' data.
