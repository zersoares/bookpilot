Starting Over After 40: Rebuilding Your Career in the Age of AI

Sources consulted on 24 October 2026.

ZMBy Zirlandia Milkovic · 2026-11-02 · 4 min read

TL;DR

  • The World Economic Forum expects 39% of workers' existing skills to change or become outdated between 2025 and 2030.
  • It also projects 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net gain of 78 million.
  • The ILO finds that for most jobs, AI is more likely to transform work than eliminate it.
  • Your experience counts. Build on it: list your skills, add AI literacy, and test small.

Starting over after 40 often comes after something else has ended: a marriage, a job, a business or a version of yourself. Then you look at the job market, and everyone is talking about AI.

Take a breath. The research is more nuanced than the headlines.

What's actually changing

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025, based on a survey of employers, projects that between 2025 and 2030:

  • 170 million jobs will be created and 92 million displaced,
  • giving a net increase of 78 million jobs, or 7% of today's total,
  • and on average 39% of workers' existing skill sets will be transformed or become outdated.

The International Labour Organization's 2025 update on generative AI found that one in four workers globally is in an occupation with some exposure to generative AI. It concludes that because most jobs include tasks that need human input, "transformation of jobs is the most likely impact."

One more finding matters here. The ILO found the highest-exposure category affects women's employment more than men's: 4.7% versus 2.4% globally. That's a reason to plan, not to panic.

What employers say they value

According to the WEF report, analytical thinking remains the most sought-after core skill. It's followed by resilience, flexibility and agility. AI and big data top the list of the fastest-growing skills.

Read that list again. Resilience and flexibility are things many people starting over have already had to build.

A five-step plan

1. Inventory what you already have

Write down everything you've done, paid or unpaid: managing a budget, resolving conflict, organising people, teaching, caring. Then translate each into the skill behind it.

2. Learn basic AI literacy

You don't need to become technical. Learn to brief an AI tool clearly, check its output and know what not to paste into it. Try it on a real task this week.

3. Think in skills, not titles

Job titles are changing fast. Ask which of your skills transfer to roles that are growing, and where AI could handle the repetitive parts.

4. Test before you leap

Take a short course, a small freelance job or a volunteer project in the field you're considering. Small experiments are cheaper than big mistakes.

5. Look after the rest of your life, too

A career rebuild goes better when your confidence, money and support network are being rebuilt alongside it.
