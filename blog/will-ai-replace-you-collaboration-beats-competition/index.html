Will AI Replace You? Why Collaboration Beats Competition

Sources consulted on 24 October 2026.

ZMBy Zirlandia Milkovic · 2026-11-03 · 4 min read

TL;DR

  • The ILO says "transformation of jobs is the most likely impact" of generative AI, because most jobs need human input.
  • The WEF projects a net gain of 78 million jobs by 2030, along with big shifts in skills.
  • Many people worry quietly: 53% of AI users at work fear it makes them look replaceable (Microsoft, 2024).
  • Don't compete with AI on its strengths. Hand it the routine parts and invest in judgement.

"Will AI replace me?" is one of the most common questions people ask about their work. The honest answer is that nobody can promise you a yes or a no. But the official research doesn't support the doom headlines either.

What the research says

Transformation more than replacement

The International Labour Organization's 2025 study found that one in four workers globally is in an occupation with some exposure to generative AI. Only 3.3% of global employment falls into the highest-exposure category. Its conclusion: "As most occupations consist of tasks that require human input, transformation of jobs is the most likely impact."

Churn, with a net gain

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 projects 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced between 2025 and 2030, a net gain of 78 million. Some roles will shrink. Employers expect clerical roles such as data entry clerks and bank tellers to be among the fastest-declining.

The quiet worry

In Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index, 53% of people who use AI at work said they worry that using it on important tasks makes them look replaceable. And 52% were reluctant to admit using it for their most important tasks.

So people use AI, but hide it. That's the opposite of collaboration.

Why competing is the wrong game

If you try to beat AI at producing a first draft in seconds or summarising a long document, you'll lose. That's not where your value is.

The WEF report says analytical thinking is still the top skill employers want, followed by resilience, flexibility and agility. Those are human strengths. They're also exactly what you need to judge and direct what an AI produces.

Four ways to collaborate

1. Split your work into tasks

List your tasks for one week. Mark each as routine (AI can draft it), shared (AI helps, you decide) or human (relationships, judgement, accountability).

2. Give AI the first draft, keep the final say

Let AI produce the starting point. You check facts, adjust tone and own the decision.

3. Use AI openly

If your team hides its AI use, nobody learns from each other. Agree as a team what's fine, what's off-limits and when to check output.

4. Invest the saved time

Spend the time you get back on what only you can do: clients, colleagues, creative ideas and the judgement calls.

A 5-minute exercise

Write down the three tasks you'd most like to stop doing and the three you'd most like to do more of. Try handing the first three to an AI tool this week, with a clear brief and a careful review.
