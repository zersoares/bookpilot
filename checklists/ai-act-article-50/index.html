Article 50 of the AI Act: the 11-point checklist for people who build sites, apps and content

Claude and Grok now watermark AI text by default, and Article 50 transparency rules have applied since 2 August 2026. The provider's mark is only part of it: chatbot notices, visible labels, logging and your own legal role are still on you. This one-page PDF turns it into 11 checks you can work through with your team before the 2 December 2026 deadline.

What's inside

  1. Inventory every place your product or site generates text, images, audio or video with AI.
  2. Identify each model provider and read its marking docs (Claude, Grok, OpenAI and others).
  3. Log the x-watermark response header (Grok), or the equivalent, per request.
  4. Don't opt out of watermarking unless a legal exception is documented.
  5. Check sampling parameters: Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6 with non-default temperature, top_p or top_k may drop the mark.
  6. Keep C2PA metadata on generated files; don't strip it in image pipelines.
  7. Chatbots: tell users they're talking to AI, from the first interaction (Art. 50(1)).
  8. Deepfakes and public-interest AI text: add a visible disclosure, unless the text was human-reviewed under editorial responsibility (Art. 50(4)).
  9. Generative systems on the market before 2 August 2026: marking by 2 December 2026 (Art. 111(4)).
  10. Publish a short AI transparency note on your site.
  11. Get legal review of your provider or deployer role. ⚠️
